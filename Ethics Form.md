**Research Project:** AI and Machine Learning in Cybersecurity Threat Detection: Perceptions of Cybersecurity and IT Participants

## My Contribution

### 1. Research Objectives

1. To investigate participants' perceptions of the ability of artificial intelligence and machine learning to detect cybersecurity threats.
2. To identify participants' views of the benefits of AI/ML-based threat detection compared with rule-based or signature-based approaches.
3. To examine challenges and constraints affecting AI/ML adoption, including data quality, false-positive alarms, adversarial attacks, skills requirements, and organisational limitations.
4. To identify measures participants believe could improve the effective and responsible use of AI/ML for cybersecurity threat detection.

### 2. Research Questions

1. What do individuals in cybersecurity and IT think about the capability of AI and ML technologies to detect cyber threats?
2. What advantages does AI/ML-based threat detection provide compared with conventional cybersecurity methods?
3. What technical, organisational, and ethical obstacles affect the deployment and effectiveness of AI/ML-based threat detection?
4. What measures are important for ensuring trustworthy and socially responsible use of AI/ML in cyber threat detection?

### 3. Research Design

The study uses a cross-sectional mixed-method survey with a mainly quantitative approach. Closed, multiple-choice, and Likert-scale questions will be used to identify patterns in perceived AI/ML effectiveness, benefits, and implementation challenges. One or two optional open-ended questions will allow participants to provide concerns or recommendations in their own words.

### 4. Participant Recruitment and Sample

Participants will be adults aged 18 years or above with a background or experience in cybersecurity, IT, information systems, networking, or related fields. Recruitment will use a short neutral message circulated through relevant student/professional networks and online channels. Participation will be voluntary and no lecturer, employer, manager, or group member will pressure anyone to participate.

The proposed sample size is **40 participants**, using convenience/purposive sampling. This is manageable within the class timeframe and suitable for descriptive analysis, while recognising that the findings will not represent the entire cybersecurity community.

### 5. Data Collection and Analysis

Data will be collected using an online questionnaire taking approximately 5–10 minutes. The questionnaire will include eligibility/background items, Likert-scale statements, multiple-choice questions, and one or two optional open-ended questions.

Closed-ended responses will be analysed using frequencies, percentages, and appropriate measures of central tendency. Open-ended responses will be reviewed using thematic analysis through familiarisation, coding, grouping related codes, developing themes, and checking themes against responses.

### 6. Voluntary Participation and Informed Consent

Participants will receive a short, plain-language information statement explaining the study purpose, eligibility, activities, expected duration, possible risks, privacy, data handling, withdrawal conditions, and researcher/supervisor contact details. Participation will be clearly described as optional.

Before accessing the questionnaire, participants will need to actively select an electronic consent statement confirming that they are aged 18 or above, have read the information, and voluntarily agree to participate. No pre-ticked consent boxes will be used.

Participants may stop the survey before submission by closing the browser and may skip optional questions without penalty. Because the survey is anonymous, participants will be informed that it may not be possible to identify and remove a response after submission.

### 7. Minimising Harm and Discomfort

The project is considered low risk. Possible risks include mild discomfort when discussing cybersecurity issues, privacy concerns, accidental disclosure of workplace or security information, reputational concerns, or perceived pressure to participate.

The questionnaire will not request employer names, exact work locations, passwords, vulnerabilities, security configurations, or identifiable incident details. Participants will be warned not to disclose sensitive operational information. Questions will remain neutral, participation will be voluntary, and sensitive questions may be skipped. Any identifying information accidentally included in open-text responses will be removed or generalised before analysis and reporting.

### 8. Confidentiality and Privacy

The study will not intentionally collect names, employer names, personal email addresses, IP addresses, or other direct identifiers. Research data will be stored in a password-protected folder on a secured device/account. Access will be limited to the student researcher and the lecturer/supervisor where supervision is required.

Research responses will not be uploaded to a public GitHub repository. Only non-sensitive assessment evidence will be uploaded where required.

The survey is intended to be anonymous. Open-ended responses will be checked for names, company names, highly specific job titles, locations, or other identifying details, and these will be removed or generalised before analysis.

### 9. Fair and Respectful Treatment

Participants will be selected based on relevance to the research topic and the requirement to be aged 18 or above. Selection will not be based on unrelated personal characteristics. Recruitment wording will be respectful and inclusive, demographic questions will be kept to a minimum, and the survey will use clear, neutral language.

All participants will receive the same study information, consent process, essential questions, and response options. Limitations arising from convenience or purposive sampling will be reported openly.

### 10. Transparency and Research Integrity

Findings will be reported accurately, with observations clearly distinguished from interpretations. Data will not be fabricated, altered, selectively omitted, or presented as more generalisable than the sample allows. The final report will describe the sampling method, participant numbers, analysis methods, limitations, and any important methodological changes.

Each group member's contribution will be acknowledged according to actual involvement. GitHub contribution evidence will be acknowledged where applicable for Week 10. Lecturer/supervisor guidance and external ideas will be appropriately acknowledged, while participants will be acknowledged collectively rather than individually.

Anonymous survey responses, datasets, and analysis files will be stored in a password-protected, access-controlled folder. Research responses will not be stored in the public GitHub repository.
